Why we exist

Cities run fast. Most things have adapted to that — food, transport, work. Two have not: getting your hair coloured properly, and getting your nails done properly. Both still cost you an afternoon in a chair.

And the afternoon is only the beginning. A gel manicure locks you into one look for weeks, leaves an awkward gap at the base as it grows out, and taking it off means going back to the salon and sitting down all over again. Do that often enough and your nails start to thin, until the lamp begins to sting.

What we make

Handmade press-on nails — shaped, painted and finished one set at a time by nail artists, never stamped out by the thousand.

They go on in about ten minutes and come off whenever you decide. No lamp, no drill, no acetone, no appointment. Applied with tabs, a set can be worn again and again, so you can keep several and choose the one that suits where you are going today rather than committing to one look for a month.

Who makes them

Our nails are made by hand by a small group of nail artists in China, most of whom work from home.

It is deliberate. Skilled nail artists are not hard to find — what is hard to find is a way to sell that skill without a salon chair and fixed hours. Working this way, they set their own schedule and stay with their families, and we get sets painted by people who chose this craft rather than people assigned to a production line. A set someone cared about looks like one.

Every design is made by our founder alongside them, which is how the standard stays the same whoever picks up the brush.

How a set reaches you

Each one is shaped to follow the curve of a natural nail, built up in layers of professional-grade gel, and finished with every pearl and charm placed by hand.

Then it is checked twice — once in the studio, once before it is boxed — for the things that decide whether a set lasts a day or two weeks: smooth edges, embellishments properly set, an even finish. Anything that does not pass does not ship.

What we won't do

No mass production. Every design is made in small numbers. When a set sells out it may be a while before it returns — sometimes not at all.

No pretending a machine did it better. Handmade means small differences in tone, in where a pearl sits, in how the light catches the top coat. That is the difference between a print and an original, and we would rather explain it than iron it out.

Made by women, for women

Everyone who makes these nails is a woman, and most of what we know came from wearing them ourselves — the ones that lifted at the edges after a day, the ones that never quite fit, the ones that were beautiful but wrecked your nails.

We are building the ones we wanted and could not find.
